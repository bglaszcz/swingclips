Swing numbers
How to read these numbers
Face-on: turns are from how much narrower the hips and shoulders look than at address; inches are scaled from the body's estimated size. Both are estimates from one camera: best for comparing your own swings. Turn + = closed (going back), - = open; tilt + = lead side higher / spine away from the target; sway + = toward the target.
Down the line: "to ball" + = moved toward the ball since address (hips + at impact = early extension). Hand height is above the middle of the shoulders, hand depth behind them (away from the ball). The plane is the shaft's line at address: hands to plane + = above it; shaft to plane + = steeper than at address (-- where the shaft was a blur and couldn't be seen).
Session trends
One dot per swing; tap one to open it; hollow = a shaky number on either axis (tracking noise or the conditions could account for it; hover for why). The line is the straight-line fit. Launch monitor: left is -, right is + (path + = in-to-out, face + = open, for a right-hander).
r runs from -1 to +1: how closely the two go up and down together (0 = no link; - = one goes up as the other goes down). The tick marks how big it must be to stand out from chance with this many swings; below that it could easily be luck. Even one that stands out is a lead to test, not proof that one causes the other.

By session
Medians per group, measured by the server from each clip (no labels). Golfer: brightness at address, 0-255 (under 70 is dark). Noise: grain in a still part of the background, in the same units (grainy from 5% of the golfer's brightness: about 5 at 100). Flicker: clips where the lights pulsed or banded at a fixed shutter, where it matters; "mild" is the same on Auto, where the longer exposure evens most of it out. Sharpness from: clips whose impact could be believed (the ball seen leaving 60 ms before to 10 ms after the heard strike); on the rest the key positions may be off, so their sharpness isn't measured. Sharp: detail along the forearms and hands at address, against their own contrast; P6 and P5-P7 are the same as a share of address in each clip, so clips in different light compare fairly: nearer 1 is sharper through the downswing. Green is the best setting for each camera. Clips from before capture app 0.4 are "unknown".

Practice
Games
The speaking phone says each target; hit it with the club you would play. Once Square's shot is in (about 15 s) it says where the ball landed against the target and the next one. A net has no roll, so the ball finishes where it lands. Each shot is scored in strokes against a tour player from that distance (+ is better); the Combine is always the same 27 shots, so its score is the one to follow week to week. Starting a game turns the practice number below off.
Past games
After each swing the speaking phone (Practice voice on, the face-on one by default) says the number and whether it was in range, e.g. "Tempo 3.2, in range" or "Club path minus 4, too far left". Body numbers come once the swing is analyzed (usually well under a minute); Square's about 15 s after the strike, or "no shot" if none came within 25 s. A body number with no reading (a camera that couldn't see all of you, the same rule as the swing numbers and the trends) or read at an estimated P6 is "no reading", not a wrong number. Numbers marked "noisy" move a lot from one swing to the next even when the swing doesn't: practice them over several swings, not one.

How it's worked out
Moves and results: each move against each result with this club, within sessions (every swing against that session's own usual, so warm-up, tiredness and where the cameras stood cancel out), pooled over the sessions in the period. With 20 moves and 16 results some links look strong by luck, so each one's chance is corrected for how many were tested (q). Strong evidence: q under 0.05 and the same way in at least 3 sessions. Worth trying: q under 0.2, not yet proven. The aim: for carry, smash and distance offline the way that helps; for path, face, curve, strike and attack angle, the way that brings your usual number toward neutral (irons: attack -4°). It describes your swings; it doesn't prove the move causes the result.
Good shots: the body numbers that differ most between this club's good shots and the rest. Strong = the 95% confidence interval of the effect size (Hedges' g) leaves out 0; with 20 numbers about one in twenty looks strong by chance. It may be the other way round (a good shot doesn't need the move).
The focus's progress: since the day it started, each session's median against the 6 sessions before, measured against how much it wobbles from session to session anyway: "clearly" = 2 wobbles or more, "maybe" = 1.5 or more. "Practice this" turns on practice mode for the move, and the phone says the swing thought after a swing out of range.
The drills and thoughts are general instruction for a right-hander; a coach watching you trumps them. Greyed ~ = mostly shaky numbers.

Gapping
Each club's median carry and middle 50% spread in this period, and the gap in median carry between neighbouring clubs. Gaps under 7 yd (overlap) or over 20 yd (big gap) are flagged. Mishits and clubs with fewer than 5 shots are left out.
Wedge matrix
How it's worked out
Swing size is the shot's club speed against that wedge's full-swing speed (the 90th percentile of all its club speeds, once there are 10): Full 92% or more, 3/4 80-92%, Half 65-80%; slower is a chip, left out. Each cell is the median carry in this period, with the middle 50% and the number of shots; it takes 3. Mishits (no carry, or marked invalid by Square) are left out. The biggest hole is the widest carry gap between the cells: a distance with no stock shot yet.
