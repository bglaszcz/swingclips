# 3D from both phones

Off by default (`SWINGCLIPS_3D=on`). Calibration, what the server does, and the checklist for the real setup. (Moved out of HOME-SETUP.md, which has the session checklist, setup and troubleshooting.)

## 3D from both phones (off by default: `SWINGCLIPS_3D=on`)
Groundwork for real 3D: with both phones calibrated, each swing's joints are triangulated from the
two views, and the page shows true pelvis and thorax turn, sway / thrust / lift in inches and a
kinematic sequence next to the face-on estimates. Nothing changes unless the server runs with
`SWINGCLIPS_3D=on` (in `Start server.cmd`: `set SWINGCLIPS_3D=on` before it starts Python): the
2D numbers, key positions, trends and pose files stay exactly as they are either way.

**Axes**: metres from the ball, **x** toward the target, **y** up, **z** toward the golfer's front
(from the golfer toward the ball and the face-on phone). Right-handed golfer only, for now.

**1. Print the boards** (`server/board.py`; print at *Actual size*, then check the 100 mm line
with a ruler):
- `python board.py lens` -> `board-lens.pdf`, one letter page (7 x 9 squares of 25 mm). Glue it
  to foam board: it has to stay flat.
- `python board.py mat` -> `board-mat.pdf`, the mat board at full size (5 x 7 squares of 150 mm,
  0.75 x 1.05 m) for a print shop (A0 or a 36" roll), or `python board.py mat --tile letter` for
  20 letter pages to trim and tape together (line up the squares on the 10 mm overlaps; glue to
  foam board). It must be this big: from hand height 3 m away the mat is seen almost edge-on
  (about 18 degrees), so a square 150 mm deep looks ~20 px tall in a 1080p frame and a marker
  bit ~3 px. If yours prints at another size, measure a square and pass `--square-mm` to
  `calib.py session`.

**From the browser** (the shed, no terminal): the **3D calibration** page (review page **Tools** menu, or linked at
the top of the Start page) does steps 2 and 3 with buttons. Per step: tap **Start**, record the board
(the phones save a clip on a clap or knock, as for a swing), tap **Calibrate**: it runs the same
`calib.py` on that recording's clips and shows its printout and verdict. During a lens recording the
phones keep 6 s before the clap (instead of 2), so wave the board for ~5 s and then clap. Clips
recorded while a calibration recording is on are tagged `calib` and left out of the trends (both
phones', since both hear the clap); **Move the clips to the trash** clears them after
(`server/calibrun.py`, `/api/calib/record`, `/api/calib/run`; the stretches are kept in
`calib\recordings.json`). The phone name comes from `calib\phones.json` (else `face-phone` /
`dtl-phone`) and can be changed on the page. 3D itself still needs `SWINGCLIPS_3D=on` and a restart;
the page says when it's off.

**2. Calibrate each phone's lens, once per phone and mode** (`server/calib.py lens`): in the mode
you swing in (1080p 240 fps high-speed crops the sensor, so its numbers aren't the 30 fps ones),
record the lens board waved slowly in front of the phone, 0.4-1 m away: tilted every way, and into
every corner and edge of the picture. The capture app records when it hears a strike, so clap to
start each 4 s clip; two or three clips is plenty. Then on the server:

    python calib.py lens --phone s23ultra --angle face D:\SwingClips\clips\swing_face_1920x1080_240fps_...mp4 ...
    python calib.py lens --phone s21 --angle dtl D:\SwingClips\clips\swing_dtl_1920x1080_240fps_...mp4 ...

It writes `D:\SwingClips\calib\s23ultra-1920x1080_240fps.json` (intrinsics, distortion, image size,
reprojection error) and notes which phone films which angle (`calib\phones.json`). It says
**good** when the RMS error is under 0.5 px, the corners reached 75% of the picture, and at least
20% of the views have the board tilted 25+ degrees from facing the phone (flat-on, the focal length
can't be told from the board's distance), and what to do about it otherwise; the exit code is 2
when it isn't good. Both phones' 240 fps video is processed so much that the corners sit 1-3 px
off the true grid (steady from frame to frame, so it isn't motion): there the RMS can't get under
0.5, so the lens is fitted with fewer numbers (one distortion term, centre in the middle, square
pixels), and good means the two halves of the recording, fitted on their own, put the same rays
within 1% of the focal length of each other (90th percentile; ~0.6 degrees, ~2 cm at 3 m), with the
RMS under 3 px. (A first version wanted 1.5 px, which a 1% focal difference alone exceeds at the
picture's edges: a recording with both halves' focal lengths 1% apart failed it.) Calibrate uses
every lens clip of that phone still in the clips folder (in the latest mode), so each try adds
views, and a good lens is never replaced by a worse try (that one is saved under `calib\tries`). The
S21's 240 fps video also blooms (white spreads a few px into black, filling in the markers' bits):
when few corners are found, the markers are read on a copy with the white shrunk by 3, 5 or 7 px,
and the corners are still found and refined on the picture as it is (`calib.detect`). On the first
real S21 recordings the board was never tilted more than 17 degrees and the two clips' focal
lengths came out 16% apart. Delete those clips afterwards (they
show up as swings). Redo it if a phone's software update changes the camera, or for another mode.

**3a. Place the cameras from your swings (no mat board)** (`server/bodycalib.py`, the calibration
page's step 3): after the tripods are on their spots, tap **Tripods are set**, hit about 10 swings,
let them be analyzed, type your height and tap **Place the cameras**. Nothing is worked out until
then. The swing worker takes the swings filmed from both phones since the tripods were set (the
newest 20, calibration clips left out), pairs their frames by the impact sync, and uses the joints
both phones are sure of while they move slowly (shoulders to wrists, hips to ankles; under 350 px/s,
since the sync is only good to a few ms):
- where the down-the-line phone stands relative to the face-on one: the essential matrix of those
  joint pairs (RANSAC), then least squares on its rotation and baseline direction, the pairs
  triangulated and put back into both pictures each step (Cauchy weights);
- the size: thigh + shin at address are 0.491 of your height; or, better, the down-the-line phone's distance
  from the ball along the floor, if you type it (tape measure from the spot under the phone). On the first
  real setup (Oct 6) the height put both phones ~11% too far (131 in for a measured 116; face-on 93 in for
  ~84); sized by the tape, the face-on phone came out at 83 in;
- up: square to both phones' picture rows (a phone on a tripod is level side to side); the target:
  along the feet at address (lead ankle minus trail, right-handed); the origin: the ball as both
  phones saw it, on the floor (between the feet if the ball wasn't found, with a warning).

It saves a session like the mat board's (`method: "body"`, with its report and the swings used),
dated when the tripods were set, so it holds for those swings and the ones after until a camera
moves (then tap **Tripods moved: set again** and hit ~10 more). The page prints each phone's height
and distance from the ball and how far apart they are: check those with a tape measure. On made-up
swings (`tests/test_bodycalib.py`; 2 px of noise per joint) the phones come out within ~2-11 cm and
~2 degrees with 6-10 swings, and the 3D joints within ~1-1.7 cm (0.2 cm with the true cameras).
Not tried on real swings yet: real joints are noisier, a tripod that isn't level side to side tilts
"up", and a stance that's open or closed turns the target line with it (the page says how far the
down-the-line phone looks off the feet line).

**3. Or each session, place the cameras with the mat board** (after the tripods are set, before the first swing): lay the
mat board flat on the mat with its centre on the ball's spot, the **x arrow pointing at the
target** and the **y arrow away from you**, stand clear, clap, and leave it ~5 s. Then:

    python calib.py session <face-on clip> <down-the-line clip>

It prints where each camera stands (face-on around z +3, down the line around x -3.5, both
0.5-2 m up), the fit in pixels, and warns when the board looks turned round; it saves
`calib\sessions\<date>_<time>.json`. With 3D on, the camera setup does the same by itself from the
phones' setup stills when a phone sees the board ("Face on: board seen", and "Both cameras are
calibrated for 3D" once both have). With 3D on, the server asks for full-size stills (capture app
0.7: the recording mode's size, e.g. 1920 x 1080, instead of ~960 px), since in tests the mat board
at 3 m was found at full resolution only. Not yet tried on the real setup: if the stills still miss
the board, use the clips.

A session's positions hold for the swings after it until a camera moves (`swing3d.py`), told two
ways: the swing's own fit at address (the core joints miss their rays by a median of more than 5 px
in the pictures; a still pair lands within ~1-2), and the ball's place in each picture against the
first 5 swings after the calibration (more than 4% of the width across or 1.5% of the height up or
down; on the first real session it held within +-2% and +-0.5%: the fit alone takes up most of a
phone turned sideways). A swing past either gets no 3D ("... camera seems to have moved ..."). The
golfer's place in the picture isn't used: it changes with the club (a 5 iron after 7 irons counted
as moved). Swings in another recording mode than the calibration get none either.

When 3 or more swings in a row since the latest calibration say a camera moved (and none after them
has 3D), `/api/calib` reports `moved` ({since, swings, text}; `app.camera_moved`): the Start page's
Get ready shows "3D off since ...", and the 3D calibration page offers **Place the cameras from those
swings**: it dates the tripods to just before the first of them (`POST /api/calib/tripods {since}`,
which must be after the latest calibration) and places the cameras from them, so the new calibration
holds for those swings too and they get their 3D back. (2026-10-04: the face-on phone's picture had
dropped ~6% between Oct 2 and Oct 4, the ball at 80% of the height instead of 86%; all 64 two-phone
swings of that session lost their 3D without a word.)

The fit uses the core joints (`tri.CORE`: shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees, ankles): on the
first real swings the head points (nose, ears) missed between the views by 14-35 px, the toes 7-21,
the trail elbow 30+ in the downswing (behind the body down the line), against 1-3 for the hips and
knees. The others are triangulated for the stick figure at less weight, a point that misses by more
than 15 px in a frame is dropped there and filled by the filter, the bones held at one length are
the core ones, the reprojection and bone numbers are the core joints', and the sub-frame sync leaves
out the trail elbow. (Before: median 6-8 px, bone spread ~23%, every swing.)

**What the server does**: for each swing with both angles, the swing worker pairs the frames by the
impact sync (each face-on frame with the down-the-line points interpolated to the same moment: the
phones drop different frames), then refines the sync within a frame and a half by where the elbows
and wrists triangulate best in the downswing (the impact frames alone are only good to ~4 ms). It
triangulates every landmark (a DLT weighted by the model's confidence), filters the swing so each
bone keeps one length and the motion is smooth (~15 Hz), and saves `<face clip>.3d.json` beside the
pose files (`/api/3d/<clip>`). Its numbers (`static/metrics3d.js`) go in the swing's record in
`swings.json` as `body3d`.

**The review page**: a **3D from both phones** panel under the swing numbers, only for calibrated
swings: a stick figure that follows the video (drag to turn it), a table at P1 / P4 / P6 / P7 with
the 2D estimate in brackets, and the kinematic sequence chart.
- **Pelvis turn**: the hip line's heading about the vertical, from address; + closed, - open.
  **Thorax turn**: the same for the shoulder line; the chest's **forward bend** toward the ball and
  **side bend** (lead shoulder up +) come from splitting its orientation (spine from mid-hips to
  mid-shoulders, shoulder line square to it) into rotation, bend, side bend. (The rotation of that
  split came out 46-108 degrees at the top on back-to-back real 7 irons; the shoulder line's
  heading 85-115.) Its top turn is the most closed one from P3 to P4: the track can jump there,
  where the arms cross the shoulders down the line (30-45 degrees in 50 ms on the first real
  swings); a jump over 25 degrees in 50 ms marks the thorax speed unreliable, left out of the
  sequence order. **X-factor**: thorax minus pelvis. **Open at impact** (+ open): pelvis and thorax,
  good players' hips ~30-45. **Pelvis side bend**: lead hip higher +. No
  pelvis forward tilt: that needs points on the front and back of the pelvis.
- **Sway / thrust / lift** (inches from address): the middle of the hips (and of the shoulders)
  toward the target, toward the ball (+ at impact = early extension) and up.
- **Kinematic sequence**: how fast the pelvis and thorax rotate toward the target, and how fast the
  lead arm (shoulder to wrist) and club (hands to clubhead) swing, from the top to just after
  impact (arm and club: 30 ms; pelvis and thorax: 120 ms, since on the first real swings they were
  still speeding up 30 ms after impact), with each one's peak. **Body late**: the pelvis or thorax at
  its fastest only after impact (the arms led the downswing); on the first real session every
  swing: hips 0-9 degrees open at impact (confirmed on the video), the lead arm fastest 33-54 ms
  before impact, the pelvis 17-42 ms after. The textbook order is pelvis, thorax, arm, club, each faster than
  the last. Speeds are filtered before the peaks are found (`metrics3d.js smoothSpeeds`, Oct 8): readings
  within 12 ms of a tracking gap over 20 ms are dropped (the clubhead jumps as it blurs away), a reading more
  than 3 robust deviations from the median of the ±25 ms around it takes that median, then a Gaussian of
  12 ms (about a 13 Hz low-pass). A segment with no reading within 20 ms of impact wasn't tracked through it
  and is left out of the order (like a jumping thorax). The pelvis start (`pelvisStartMs`) keeps the speeds
  as measured, as its 20 deg/s threshold was set on them. On the 6 fixtures: same findings (arms first, body
  late), two false club peaks gone (154 and 179 ms before impact). The club only shows with the club model on (`SWINGCLIPS_CLUB_BACKEND=yolo`, it has the
  clubhead); the **club check** then compares hands-to-clubhead with the club's standard length
  (less 4.5" of grip above the hands): more than 10% off means the calibration or the clubhead
  points are off.
- The line above the figure says how well it fits: the **reprojection error** (the 3D points put
  back into each picture, median px), how much **bone lengths varied** before the filter (a few %
  is normal; 15%+ means one view is off), and how far the sync moved from the impact frames.
- **Setup check and missing numbers**: Every 3D number in the table is measured from setup
  (`metrics3d.compute`: turns against pelvis / thorax at address, slides against base at address).
  `tri.swing` drops a joint wherever the two cameras miss by more than 15 px (`OUTLIER_PX`), and one
  joint missing at setup blanks its whole row. `metrics3d.compute` checks the still stretch before the
  swing (0.4 s before P1 to takeaway, or P1 + 0.1 s without one) for core joints missing in all frames
  of that window (`setupMissing`):
  - When a joint is missing at setup, the panel says plainly why in a muted note above the table,
    with no P-numbers (e.g. `The lead shoulder wasn't seen by both cameras at setup, so shoulder turn,
    tilt and the chest can't be measured on this swing (the cameras' calibration is off by more than
    15 px there). The swing order below is from the downswing and still holds.`).
  - Rows blanked show `not at setup` in a muted style instead of `--`, keeping 2D estimates in brackets.
  - When nothing at all was seen before takeaway, the note says `No 3D at setup on this swing: ...`
    and the stick figure displays from the first moment with a body, with `3D from <time>` underneath
    instead of an empty box.

**Calibration health (mat board vs swings)**: `/api/calib` reports `health` for the latest calibration
session and the one before it (`swings`, `lostAtSetup`, `mostLost`, `typical` median address reprojection
error and `worstTenth` 90th percentile), and `calibrate.html` displays a summary line near the top:
e.g. `Oct 6, 2:58 PM (from swings): 25 of 78 swings lost the lead shoulder at setup; setup error 3.3 px typical, 13 px worst tenth. Oct 2 (mat board): 0 of 40; 2.7 / 7 px.`
- **Finding**: on the Oct 2 mat-board calibration, 0 of 40 swings lost a joint at setup (typical error
  2.7 px, worst tenth 7 px). Placing cameras from swings (`method: "body"`) produces a looser fit at
  setup: on Oct 6 at 2:58 PM, 25 of 78 swings lost the lead shoulder at setup and 10 lost hips (typical
  3.3-3.5 px, worst tenth 11-13 px); on Oct 6 at 1:52 PM, 9 of 14 lost hips.
- When more than 1 in 5 swings (> 20%) lose a joint at setup, the page warns: `3D is missing numbers on
  many swings: recalibrate with the mat board (step 3)` linking directly to the mat board step.

**Scorecard**: with 3D on, `eval.py` adds each swing's reprojection error and bone-length spread,
and for swings labeled on both angles, the 3D joints put back into each view against the labels,
beside the 2D tracker's error on the same joints.

**Not tested on real clips yet** (all of it was built against made-up cameras and a made-up swing:
`tests/test_3d.py`). To check on the real setup, in order:
1. Print both boards; the 100 mm line measures 100 mm.
2. Lens calibration on a real 240 fps clip from each phone comes out **good** (RMS under 0.5 px).
   Motion blur at 1/240 s may need slower waving or the fixed 1/1000 shutter.
3. The mat board is found in both phones' full-resolution clips at your distances, and the camera
   positions printed match a tape measure (within a few cm), with no "turned round" warning.
4. On real swings: reprojection error under ~5 px, bone spread under ~10%, and the sync correction
   within ±4 ms. Pelvis and thorax turn at the top in a plausible range (pelvis ~40-50, thorax
   ~85-100 for a full swing), and the kinematic sequence order stable across swings of one club.
5. Moving a tripod between swings stops 3D for the later ones.
6. A label pass on both angles of a few swings: the 3D joints land on the labels about as well as
   the 2D tracker or better.
7. Whether the setup stills find the board (full size with 3D on, capture app 0.7): if not, the
   capture app needs a "calibrate" recording for the voice prompt to work.
